## Register 1. Doubts About Assumptions (Undermining Doubts)

These questions concern the premises of an argument.
Even if a theorem is correctly proved, can we establish the assumptions necessary to apply it?

## Register 2. Doubts About Conclusions (Rebutting Doubts)

These questions concern whether a claimed conclusion is false.
For example, a counterexample satisfying all the premises
but violating the conclusion would challenge the claimed theorem or its formalization.
A model that violates a required assumption does not
necessarily refute a conditional theorem.
If a counterexample is found,
we must identify the exact claim it challenges and
whether its assumptions are satisfied.

**None currently identified.**

## Register 3. Doubts About What Our Results Mean (Undercutting Doubts)

These questions concern the connection
between a formal theorem and the scientific claim made about it.
A theorem may be correct while its informal interpretation is too strong.
Some of these doubts are addressed by permanent documentation
rules rather than additional proofs.

### UC-001. (STANDING RULES) Agreement, Sameness, and Distinctness

#### Research criterion

Scientific Adequacy

#### Concern

A reader might read agreement on descriptors as identity, or failure to show sameness as distinctness.

#### Current Knowledge

Agreement on observed properties or structural descriptors does not establish identity of the intended referents.
Distinct referents may agree on every recorded descriptor.
Failure to establish sameness from available evidence does not establish distinctness.

#### Standing Rules

1. Do not present agreement on descriptors as establishing identity.
2. Do not present failure to establish sameness as establishing distinctness.

### UC-002. (STANDING RULES) Same-Unit Within the Construction

#### Research criterion

Scientific Adequacy

#### Concern

A reader might read a same-unit result as holding beyond the construction.

#### Current Knowledge

Support equality establishes same-unit only within this construction.
This does not alter the formal same-or-distinct result for successful presentations within the exclusive-whole construction.

#### Standing Rules

1. State the co-membership criterion and support interpretation with every same-unit or distinct-unit result.
